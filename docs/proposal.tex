\begin{abstract}
The FLAME benchmark is the first standardised
federated-learning (FL) testbed for robotic manipulation, but its
CNN+MLP behaviour-cloning baseline collapses on three of four tasks
(0\% success on \emph{Insert} and \emph{Scoop}, 24--28\% on
\emph{Slide}). We argue this is a \emph{representation} and
\emph{action-distribution} failure, not an aggregation one:
the encoder overfits to per-environment textures and the
deterministic MLP averages over multi-modal demos. We propose
a frozen \textbf{DINOv3 + Depth-Anything backbone with LoRA},
a \textbf{conditional diffusion head with split gripper}, and
\textbf{LoRA-only FL aggregation}. Same-env probes lift
\emph{Slide Block} from 0.24 to 0.75 SR; a 10-round federated pilot
confirms the diffusion head trains stably under FedAvg.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and Gap}
\label{sec:intro}

Cross-institution data sharing for robot demonstrations is bottlenecked
by privacy, bandwidth, and operator-policy constraints, making
federated learning (FL) a natural fit. FLAME~\cite{flame2025}
operationalises this for manipulation: 4 RLBench tasks $\times$ 420
perturbed environments $\times$ 100 demos ($\sim$15\,M samples), with
a FLOWER-based FL pipeline. Yet its reported online success rates
(Tab.~\ref{tab:flame}) reveal that the canonical
``CNN encoder + MLP head + FedAvg'' recipe \emph{fails} on the
manipulation tasks that actually require precise contact-rich behaviour.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering

\caption{Online success rate of FLAME baselines (from
Tab.~II of~\cite{flame2025}).}
\label{tab:flame}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Method & Slide & Close & Peg & Scoop\\
\midrule
FedAvg  & 0.24 & 0.84 & 0.00 & 0.00\\
FedAvgM & 0.00 & 0.54 & 0.00 & 0.00\\
FedOpt  & 0.28 & 0.70 & 0.00 & 0.00\\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The paper attributes the gap to environmental heterogeneity (background,
lighting, viewpoint, object texture) and to a mismatch between offline
RMSE and online success. We argue more concretely that
\emph{(i)} a 3-layer CNN trained from scratch on 100 demos per client
cannot acquire texture/lighting-invariant features, and
\emph{(ii)} an MSE-trained MLP averages over the multi-modal action
distribution intrinsic to manipulation, producing physically incoherent
trajectories. Standard FL algorithms cannot repair either deficit.

\noindent\textbf{Hypothesis.} The combination of
(a)~a frozen self-supervised vision backbone with low-rank
client-side adaptation, (b)~a diffusion-based action head, and
(c)~an FL strategy that only aggregates the lightweight adapters,
yields a substantially stronger and more communication-efficient
baseline for federated manipulation.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We retain FLAME's task suite, environment splits, and FLOWER pipeline so
that all comparisons are apples-to-apples. We replace three components
of the local agent.

\subsection{Vision: Frozen DINOv3 + Depth-Anything + LoRA}
The RGB stream is encoded by a frozen DINOv3 ViT-S/16~\cite{dinov2}. We insert
LoRA~\cite{lora} adapters of rank $r\!\in\!\{4,8\}$ in the last
$N\!=\!8$ attention blocks; the rest of the backbone is shared, byte-identical,
across clients and is therefore \emph{not} subject to FL drift.
A frozen monocular Depth-Anything~\cite{depthanything} Small branch provides a 3-D geometry
prior that is concatenated to DINO tokens before pooling, recovering
viewpoint cues that 2-D features alone miss. The 8-DoF
proprioceptive state is fused via gated global FiLM, letting the robot
pose condition the visual interpretation.

\subsection{Action: Conditional Diffusion Head}
The deterministic 4-unit MLP of the FLAME baseline is replaced by a
20-step DDPM denoising MLP~\cite{diffpolicy} conditioned on the joint
(visual, state) embedding. We adopt action chunking with
$\text{chunk}=4,\ \text{exec}=2$ (receding-horizon execution), and
predict the gripper command with a separate sigmoid+BCE head, since the
arm action is multi-modal but the gripper is essentially a deterministic
binary event. We support both joint-velocity (the FLAME interface) and
joint-position-direct action targets; preliminary replay-ceiling
analyses show that JV is sufficient for \emph{Slide} and \emph{Close
Box}, while \emph{Insert} and \emph{Scoop} require JP-direct to
recover the demonstration ceiling.

\subsection{FL Strategy: LoRA-Only Aggregation}
Only the LoRA matrices, the visual projector, the proprio MLP, and the
diffusion head are sent to the server, totalling
$\sim$10\% of the model parameters. The frozen DINOv3 + Depth-Anything
backbone is never communicated nor updated, eliminating the dominant
source of client drift. We will benchmark FedAvg~\cite{fedavg}, FedProx,
and SCAFFOLD~\cite{scaffold} on this reduced surface; an early pilot (Tab.~\ref{tab:fl})
already confirms that the diffusion head trains stably in FL and that
FedProx's proximal term \emph{hurts} when only adapters are aggregated.

50 epochs on \texttt{env0}; FL pilots use 10 rounds, fraction-fit 0.05.

The same-env table demonstrates that swapping the head from MLP to
diffusion (DINO frozen $\to$ +Diffusion) is the decisive lift
($+0.30$~SR), while LoRA adaptation and the depth branch each add a
further few points. The FL pilot confirms the design transfers to the
distributed setting and that lightweight FedAvg is preferable to
FedProx on this aggregation surface, contradicting the common wisdom
from highly non-IID image classification.

\section{Plan and Deliverables}
\label{sec:plan}

\textbf{P1 Same-env (wk 1--2).} 4-task sweep with the best
DINO+Depth+Diffusion config, JP-direct for \emph{Insert/Scoop}, and
a 100-epoch split-gripper config for \emph{Close Box}.
Scale $|E|\!\in\!\{5,10\}$ to map the generalisation curve.

\textbf{P2 Federated baseline (wk 3--4).} Best same-env config under
FLAME's official protocol (30 rounds, 50 local epochs, 20/400 clients)
to reproduce Tab.~\ref{tab:flame}. Ablate local-epochs
$\in\{1,5,10,25\}$ and LoRA rank $\in\{2,4,8\}$.

\textbf{P3 FL strategies (wk 5--6).} Add SCAFFOLD and FedBN on the
LoRA-only surface; isolate which aggregator benefits most from a
frozen backbone.

\textbf{P4 Final tables (wk 7).} 4-task$\times$5-method comparison,
ablation over \{vision, head, LoRA, fusion\}, and client-count scaling
(5/20/50/100/400). Stretch: VolumeDP-lite 3-D spatial-token decoder.

\section{Expected Contributions}
\label{sec:contrib}
\textbf{C1.} A foundation-model visual front-end that removes
per-client backbone drift and cuts FL communication by $\sim$10$\times$.\;
\textbf{C2.} First evidence that a diffusion policy head trains
stably and beats deterministic baselines under FL, with a separate
arm/gripper head.\;
\textbf{C3.} An empirical study on FLAME of how LoRA-only aggregation
interacts with FedAvg, FedProx, and SCAFFOLD, released as a
reproducible recipe.
